# Verified protocol

Simple Touch samples use 40 kbit/s binary FSK near 433.92 MHz, approximately 20 kHz deviation and sync `2DD4`. The measured center is subject to receiver calibration. The firmware defaults to 433.925 MHz; one tested CC1101 needed a configured 433.934850 MHz to achieve the desired actual center. Calibration is specific to hardware.

After sync: `12 | 12 C1 | encrypted body (16 bytes) | CRC (2 bytes)`.

- `12`: length of flags plus encrypted body, excluding the length and CRC.
- `12 C1`: one-way flags.
- Body: counter (2), source (4), destination (4), group mask (2), payload (4).
- Body integers are serialized big-endian. Source equals destination for our remote samples, group mask `0001`.
- Encryption: XXTEA variant, 14 rounds for four little-endian 32-bit words. Key bytes are ASCII `DOOYA 20141625` plus two zero bytes.
- CRC: polynomial `8005`, initial `FFFF`, non-reflected, xorout zero; computed over flags and ciphertext, transmitted big-endian.

| Action | Payload sequence |
| --- | --- |
| P2 | `02 02 0A 00` |
| Up | `02 02 01 00`, then `02 02 0B 00` |
| Down | `02 02 02 00`, then `02 02 0C 00` |
| Stop | `02 02 03 00` |
| Favorite position | `02 02 13 00` |

Each distinct packet repeats three times. Counters increment between distinct packets. The exact meaning of the `0B`/`0C` follow-ups has not been established; preserve the observed sequences. Replay worked on the tested shade, so a counter's presence does not establish strict anti-replay enforcement.

The firmware uses RF envelope templates derived from captures owned by the project contributor. Identity and command bytes are generated at runtime; no physical remote identity is built into the firmware. New addresses are random nonzero 24-bit values with a trailing zero byte, matching the tested address shape. Collisions are checked locally but cannot be ruled out against all nearby physical remotes.

All 48 unique captured packets from three remotes decrypted consistently and regenerated byte-for-byte. A separate generated identity was accepted by the motor and controlled Up, Down and Stop. This proves the tested protocol path, not universal motor compatibility or knowledge of sticker-number assignment.

A held STOP sent repeated Stop packets, then three Favorite packets after roughly two seconds. A generated Favorite packet from the paired virtual identity recalled the saved position in a user-observed test. The bridge sends the Favorite action directly; it does not emulate a long button hold or expose the extended hold used for programming.

Reference: [blindding](https://codeberg.org/abrasive/blindding), inspected revision `cce9d397e1b747f0697a749f64014a83621550a6`. Its published Dooya variant was the decisive research lead. Its bridge-pairing flow is different from the physical-remote P2 flow used here.
